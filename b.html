  • Chinook Salmon. Astoria's Chinook show a spring run building through April-June (spring Chinook staging at the Columbia mouth) and a dominant fall run peaking late July through August as returning adults stack up in the estuary before the September upriver push. Abundance drops sharply through fall and bottoms out in winter when few adults remain in these lower-river waters.
  • Coho Salmon. Coho are absent inshore through winter, with a modest late-spring pulse of feeder/ocean fish building along the Columbia mouth from June-July. Peak biological abundance hits late August through mid-September (weeks 34-37) as adult coho stage off Astoria before the fall river run, tailing off sharply by late October once fish push upriver and out of nearshore waters.
  • Halibut. Pacific halibut move onto the nearshore banks and Columbia River mouth grounds off Astoria in spring as they migrate from deeper offshore winter spawning habitat, peaking roughly March–May when the largest concentrations stage on the shelf. Abundance stays elevated through summer while feeding, then declines through fall as fish retreat to deeper offshore waters for winter, leaving low but non-zero presence in the accessible fishery zone during December–February.
  • Lingcod. Lingcod are resident, structure-oriented fish near Astoria's nearshore reefs and jetties year-round, so abundance never hits zero. The strongest concentration is late winter through early summer (Feb–June) when males guard nests in shallow rocky habitat and fish move up onto reefs, with a secondary bump in early fall as post-spawn fish feed heavily before winter dispersal to deeper water.
  • Dungeness Crab. Dungeness crab are resident year-round in the Astoria/Columbia estuary and nearshore, so abundance never hits zero, but the curve dips in late spring/early summer (weeks 12-18) when adult males molt to soft-shell and are lighter and less abundant on the grounds. Peak abundance and quality occur late summer through fall (weeks 33-40) once crabs have hardened and filled out, sustaining strong numbers into the winter commercial season.
  • Black Rockfish. Black rockfish are non-migratory reef residents present year-round off Astoria, so the curve stays broad and flat with no true zeros. Abundance peaks in summer (Jul–Aug) when adults move shallower to feed and warmer, calmer nearshore water concentrates fish, with a slight winter dip as some fish hold deeper during storms.
  • Rockfish (Aggregate). Rockfish are resident, long-lived structure-oriented fish present in Astoria's nearshore reefs and ocean waters year-round, so the curve stays broad with no true biological absence. Abundance peaks in summer (June–August) when calmer seas, warmer water, and aggregation over reefs concentrate fish nearshore, while winter storms and offshore dispersal thin nearshore presence to a lower baseline.

Fishing Astoria

Astoria sits at the mouth of the Columbia River on Oregon's north coast, with access to a spread of offshore humps and rocky structure along the outer coast toward Tillamook Rock. The waters here hold Chinook Salmon, which peak from March through April and again from May into September, alongside Pacific Halibut that run strong from March through August and Lingcod that turn on from January through March. Most of the fishing spots cluster around exposed offshore humps and reef features scattered across the open water off the headlands, where depth and bottom structure concentrate fish. Coho Salmon extend the salmon calendar from July into October, keeping the fishery active through the summer and early fall. Anglers targeting Chinook here work these waters primarily by trolling.
